# Glossary

Abbreviations and terms used in this project, roughly grouped. Add new ones as they appear.

## Regulation and registers

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **EU CTR** | EU Clinical Trials Regulation (EU) No 536/2014. Applies since 31 Jan 2022; replaced the old Clinical Trials Directive. Requires sponsors to post results. |
| **CTIS** | Clinical Trials Information System, the EU's single portal and public register for trials under the CTR, run by the EMA ([euclinicaltrials.eu](https://euclinicaltrials.eu)). Our main data source. |
| **EMA** | European Medicines Agency, the EU drug regulator that operates CTIS. |
| **EudraCT** | The older EU trials database (before the CTR). Trials still running in Jan 2025 had to move ("transition") to CTIS. |
| **EU CT number** | CTIS trial identifier, e.g. `2025-522750-39-00` (year, number, check digits, version). |
| **Transitioned trial** | A trial started under the old Directive/EudraCT and moved to CTIS. |
| **EEA** | European Economic Area: EU countries plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway. CTIS marks trials as "EEA only" or "EEA and non-EEA". |
| **MSC** | Member State Concerned: an EU/EEA country where the trial runs. |
| **RMS** | Reporting Member State: the MSC that leads the assessment of the application. |
| **Part I / Part II** | The two halves of a CTIS application. Part I = scientific content assessed jointly (protocol, product, endpoints); Part II = national aspects per country (consent forms, recruitment, sites). |
| **Sponsor** | The organisation responsible for the trial (company, hospital, university). Legally responsible for posting results. |

## Trials

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Phase I–IV** | Trial stages. I: first in humans/safety; II: does it work, dose finding; III: confirm benefit vs standard care; IV: after approval. Bruckner et al. studied Phases II–IV. |
| **IMP** | Investigational Medicinal Product: the drug being tested. |
| **Endpoint / outcome** | What a trial measures to judge the result (e.g. survival at 12 months). **Primary** = main question; **secondary** = extra questions. CTIS says "endpoint", publications usually say "outcome". |
| **End of trial** | When the trial finishes in a country. **Global end of trial** = finished everywhere; the results deadline counts from this date. |
| **Completion date** | The later of a trial's end date and its global end date in CTIS (Bruckner et al.'s rule); results deadlines count from it. |
| **Early termination** | Trial stopped before planned, with a reason (e.g. sponsor decision). Still has to report results. |
| **Temporary halt** | Trial paused; may restart. |
| **Paediatric trial** | A trial under the paediatric regulation: it recruits children (age group 0-17 years or in utero) or has a PIP. Shorter results deadline (6 months instead of 12). |
| **PIP** | Paediatric Investigation Plan: an EMA-agreed plan for testing a medicine in children. |
| **SmPC** | Summary of Product Characteristics: the official prescribing information of an approved medicine. |

## Results

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Summary of results** | The structured scientific results the sponsor must upload to CTIS (usually a PDF). The core thing we check. |
| **Lay (layperson) summary** | Plain-language results summary for the public, also required. |
| **CSR** | Clinical Study Report: the full, long report submitted to regulators; published in CTIS only in some cases (document type 45, our kind study-report). |
| **Due** | A trial whose results deadline has passed. Bruckner et al.: completed more than 12 months (paediatric: 6) before the export day. |
| **On time / late** | Results posted before / after the deadline. Bruckner et al. count days: within 365 (paediatric: 183) days of completion is on time. |
| **Reported fully** | Bruckner et al.'s definition: a full scientific results document AND a layperson summary posted. **Days to results** = results publication date minus completion date, for trials reported fully. |
| **Not fully reported** | Results missing, or posted but missing required parts (e.g. participant numbers or primary outcome results). |
| **Extended** | Deadline legitimately moved later (e.g. justified deferral). Not a violation. |
| **Outcome switching** | Publishing different outcomes than were registered (dropping or adding primary outcomes) without saying so. |

## Publications

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Europe PMC** | Free database of life-science papers ([europepmc.org](https://europepmc.org)); we search it for each trial's journal article. |
| **PMID / PMCID** | PubMed ID / PubMed Central ID: identifiers of a paper and of its free full text. |
| **DOI** | Digital Object Identifier: permanent link to a paper or dataset. |

## Research and related projects

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bruckner et al. 2026** | The study our benchmark comes from: manual check of CTIS reporting compliance (277 due trials, 234 legally required to report). |
| **OSF** | Open Science Framework ([osf.io](https://osf.io)), where Bruckner et al. published their data and code. |
| **TranspariMED** | Till Bruckner's campaign for clinical-trial transparency. |
| **TrialScout** | LLM tool that links publications to trial registrations (Ahnström et al. 2026). |
| **EU TrialsTracker** | Oxford tracker of results reporting in the old EudraCT register. |
| **COMPare** | Oxford project that checked published trials for outcome switching; its labels are our benchmark for that check. |
| **CC BY 4.0** | Creative Commons Attribution licence: reuse is allowed if the source is credited. |

## Project and tech

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TrialWatch** | This project; code at [github.com/shmarlya/trialwatch](https://github.com/shmarlya/trialwatch), docs at [shmarlya.github.io/trialwatch](https://shmarlya.github.io/trialwatch/). |
| **Snapshot** | A stored copy of what a source said about a trial at one point in time: a trial record version (core.trial_record); a new one is saved only when the content changes. |
| **Published judgement** | A judgement released to the public (checks.publication, ADR-007): a moderator or admin reviewed it on the trial page, or its run published it because its confidence was at least 90%. The public home page counts these (the public sees no other page for now); a withdrawn one is kept but no longer counted. |
| **Confidence (of a judgement)** | The share of gold items that judgements of the same kind agreed with: same model, prompt text and requirement set, same label, and the same evidence signs (every quote verified, asked again with every page, a question unanswered). Measured over at least 20 items, else not measured (checks/publishing.py). |
| **Gold evaluation** | Our whole workflow (transfer from CTIS, evidence check, scoring) run on a gold dataset's labelled trials, passing or failing on the gates in evals/gates.json: `trialwatch gold eval`, run by the short (hardest 50) and full CI pipelines. |
| **Frozen snapshot** | CTIS's raw answers (records and document files) for a gold dataset's trials, recorded once and replayed to the CTIS adapter, so every gold evaluation reads the same evidence. A .tar.gz in a GitHub release, named with its sha256 by evals/snapshot.lock.json. |
| **Gold labels** | Hand-checked answers (from Bruckner, COMPare) we compare our results against. Kept only as gold datasets: files in data/gold/, in git, never in the database. |
| **Evals** | Scoring our checks against gold labels: `trialwatch gold compare` scores one check run against a gold dataset, and a gold evaluation runs the whole workflow and fails when a gate in evals/gates.json is missed. |
| **Reference data** | Small, fixed lookup lists that the migration loads: identifier schemes (core.identifier_scheme) and document kinds (core.document_kind). CTIS's own code lists (status, phase, region) are not stored: its document types are mapped to our kinds in src/trialwatch/sources/ctis.py, and its other codes stay in the record as received. |
| **Migration** | A numbered SQL file that changes the database schema, applied in order by dbmate (the migrate service in compose.yaml), which then writes db/schema.sql. On 2026-11-21 the earlier migrations were squashed into one file, db/migrations/20261121120000_init.sql (nothing is in production yet). A database made before it is reset with `docker compose down -v` (which deletes its data), not migrated. New changes get new files. |
| **LLM** | Large language model (here: Claude), used to read PDFs and match publications. |
| **MCP** | Model Context Protocol: a standard way for AI assistants to call tools; we plan an MCP server exposing our findings. |
| **CI** | Continuous Integration: automated checks run on every code change. Ours run on GitHub Actions on every push and pull request: .github/workflows/tests.yml runs the whole test suite against a Postgres service of its own, and .github/workflows/docs.yml runs the docs check and builds the docs site (and publishes it from main). Started by hand, and calling CTIS or the model: .github/workflows/eval-short.yml and eval-full.yml (gold evaluations, sharing eval-shared.yml) and gold-snapshot.yml (records the frozen snapshot). |
| **Test database** | Where the tests run: `<database>_test` on the same Postgres server, made afresh from db/migrations/*.sql for every test session by tests/conftest.py, never the working database. CTIS and the model are faked, so the tests make no calls. Without a reachable server the database tests are skipped. |
| **Data-access layer** | src/trialwatch/db/: the only place with SQL. db/__init__.py opens connections (`connect()`: UTC, and who is writing); one module per schema (db/core.py, db/checks.py, db/auth.py, db/costs.py, db/jobs.py) and db/overview.py for the web interface's read-only pages, with named functions that return typed rows (dataclasses). The sources, checks, the CLI and the web interface call these functions; tests/test_sql_lives_in_db.py fails if SQL appears anywhere else. |
| **Central model** | ADR-003: trials and documents stored the same way whatever their source, in schema core (db/migrations/20261121120000_init.sql; queries in src/trialwatch/db/core.py). Each source has an adapter that fills it (src/trialwatch/sources/): CTIS, and the manual source for what a person enters. Gold labels are not in it: they are files in data/gold/. |
| **Ingest run** | One run of a source adapter (core.ingest_run), of kind `list` (syncing a source's list) or `transfer` (transferring evidence): what it was asked and the source's config it used, its counts and errors, and how it ended. Its audit entries carry the note "ingest run N". One process at a time syncs or transfers from a source (an advisory lock); a second one is refused until the first has finished. |
| **Sync** | Syncing a source's list (ADR-005, `trialwatch source sync CODE` or Sync list on its page): read every trial the source lists, as its config filters them, and match each to a trial here by its key and identifiers (or create it), with what the list says in core.source_listing. Nothing but the list is fetched. CTIS answers at most 10,000 rows per query, so a longer list is read in EU CT number order from both ends: ascending, then descending until it reaches the trials the ascending pass read. A trial seen twice in one pass is skipped (the list shifted while it was read). A trial is marked no longer listed (kept) only when two complete syncs in a row missed it. See Evidence transfer for the records and documents. |
| **Source listing** | What a source's list says about a trial it lists (core.source_listing): its title, the day the source last changed it, the listed row as given, when it was first and last listed, and when it was marked no longer listed (two complete list syncs in a row missed it). A copy of the source's list, rewritten by each sync (not in the audit log). |
| **Evidence transfer** | Bringing one trial's evidence in from a source (ADR-005, `trialwatch source transfer CODE`, Transfer on a source's or trial's page; src/trialwatch/sources/stages.py): its full record, the documents of the kinds the source's config names and their page text. CTIS: `evidence.document_kinds`, by default the summary of results and the clinical study report; lay summaries only if the config asks (they are often one per language, and no check reads them). A document the source no longer lists is unlinked from the trial (kept). core.evidence_transfer records the transfer as `complete` (everything came in and was read) or `failed` (with what), and the document kinds it brought. While a trial is being transferred its row says failed, so an interrupted transfer never looks complete. Chosen by key, or all `pending` (listed, never transferred), `failed`, `stale` or `listed` trials. |
| **Evidence state** | A trial's state at a source (view core.evidence_status): not transferred, failed, stale (complete, but the source changed the trial since) or complete. The source's list gives the day of a trial's last change, not the time, so a trial counts as stale when that day is the transfer's day or later: a trial changed on the day of its transfer stays stale until a transfer on a later day, since the change may have come after it (db/migrations/20261121180000_detect_changes.sql). A check reads a trial only when every source that holds it is complete and current, and its transfer brought every document kind the check reads that the source can give (not_ready in src/trialwatch/sources/stages.py). A stale trial is transferred again first: otherwise a sponsor's corrected document would be judged by its old copy (ADR-005, amended 2026-11-21). The evidence check, the CLI and the web interface refuse the others and say why. A trial entered by hand is complete once its uploaded documents are read. |
| **Manual source** | Trials and documents a person enters (src/trialwatch/sources/manual.py): the web interface's "Add a trial" form (identifiers, title, sponsor, status, primary endpoints, documents) and "Upload a document" on a trial page. Identifiers are matched first, so a known trial is never added twice; a trial without a registry identifier gets our own TW-number. Only PDF files are taken. Recorded with the person as actor, linked by hand (basis manual). Like any source it can be switched off; then nothing is added. |
| **Source** | Where data comes from (core.source): a registry such as CTIS, a publication index, or manual entry. A registered source names the adapter that reads it (src/trialwatch/sources/registry.py) and holds its config, one current one, checked by the adapter: what its list holds and which evidence a transfer brings. It can be switched off (`trialwatch source config CODE --disable`, or on its page). Every stored record carries (source, the source's own key), unique, so fetching it again adds a version instead of a duplicate. |
| **Actor** | Who changed data (core.actor): a person (their login name, or TRIALWATCH_ACTOR; in the web interface, the signed-in user, whose auth.app_user row points at their actor) or a process. Actor 1 is the process `trialwatch`, TrialWatch itself: migrations, seeding the first admin, scheduled jobs, the weekly refresh's checks and what a run publishes by itself (`db.connect_system()`). `trialwatch.db.connect(actor=...)` names it for the session; writes to the central tables without one are refused. |
| **Role** | What a signed-in user of the web interface is (ADR-006): `admin` or `moderator`, one per user, stored in auth.app_user.role. Admins may do every action; moderators curate trials and their evidence. What each role may do is the action chart, not data. Anyone not signed in ("public") sees only the home page and the sign-in page. |
| **Action** | Something the web interface lets a person do, named in the action chart (src/trialwatch/auth/actions.py): `view`, `trial.create`, `check.run`, `source.configure`, `user.manage`, … Every route that changes something names its action (`Depends(Requires(...))`) and is refused (401 when not signed in, 403 when the role may not) otherwise; tests/test_auth.py fails if a changing route names none. /admin/roles shows the chart. |
| **Session** | A signed-in browser (auth.session): a random token in the HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie `tw_session`, stored only as its SHA-256. It ends after 12 hours unused, after 7 days, at sign-out, and when the user's role or password changes or they are disabled. Users sign in with their email (lower case, unique; also their actor name in the audit log). They are added by an admin (/admin/users), with `trialwatch user add`, or, for the first admin, from TRIALWATCH_ADMIN_EMAIL and TRIALWATCH_ADMIN_PASSWORD when `trialwatch web` starts (only if no user has that email; src/trialwatch/auth/accounts.py); there is no sign-up. |
| **Audit log** | core.audit_log: every insert, update and delete on the central tables, with actor, time, the row before and after, written by Postgres triggers in the same transaction. Never updated, deleted or truncated (triggers refuse it). Answers "who added this trial or document, and when". |
| **Job** | A workflow step queued in jobs.job (sync, transfer, check, or the weekly refresh) and run by `trialwatch worker` (src/trialwatch/jobs/), one at a time, as whoever created it. Status: queued, running, waiting (for its child jobs), held (by the spending cap), succeeded, failed, cancelled. A failed attempt is tried again after a growing pause, up to its max attempts; then it stays failed for a person (/admin/jobs). Runs and billed calls record the job they were made for (job_id). |
| **Weekly refresh** | The job the weekly-refresh schedule (jobs.schedule) starts as TrialWatch: sync every source's list, transfer what is new, stale or failed, then check the trials never judged or whose judgement is outdated (src/trialwatch/jobs/refresh.py). |
| **Spend** | What paid API calls cost: one core.api_call row per billed model attempt (a fallback's attempts priced at the model that ran each), with tokens, cost in US dollars and the prices used, from src/trialwatch/costs/prices.json. Shown per call, judgement, run and job, and in total on /admin/spend. |
| **Worker** | `trialwatch worker` (src/trialwatch/jobs/worker.py; the `worker` service in compose.yaml): runs queued jobs one at a time, each as whoever created it, with a heartbeat; tries failed ones again after a growing pause, starts the jobs schedules make, and puts held jobs back in the queue when the spending cap allows. |
| **Schedule** | A recurring job in jobs.schedule, started by the worker when due. The first is `weekly-refresh`: sync every source, transfer new, stale and failed trials, and check only trials never judged or with an outdated judgement (src/trialwatch/jobs/refresh.py). |
| **Prices** | src/trialwatch/costs/prices.json: what each model costs per million tokens. Every billed call's cost is its tokens times these prices, stored with the prices used; a new model needs a price before it runs. |
| **Monthly cap** | The most automatic checks may spend in a calendar month (UTC), set by an admin on /admin/spend (core.app_setting). At the cap, automatic check jobs are held, not failed; a person's check always runs. |
| **Trial identifier** | An id of a trial in some scheme (eu-ct, eudract, nct, isrctn, who-utn, or trialwatch for trials added by hand), normalised and unique in core.trial_identifier, so it always points to one trial. Incoming records are matched to trials by all their identifiers. A value that is not valid for its scheme (e.g. an NCT number given as "N/A") is skipped. From CTIS: the EU CT number, the EudraCT number (authorizedApplication.eudraCt.eudraCtCode, or an "EudraCT" entry among the other registries), and the NCT, WHO UTN and ISRCTN numbers. |
| **Trial identity** | Which trial a source's record belongs to (src/trialwatch/db/core.py). A source's key always belongs to one trial (core.trial_source), and a new key is never attached to a trial that already has another key at the same source: an extension or resubmission that names its parent's NCT number becomes its own trial, plus a possible duplicate. One process at a time decides identities (an advisory lock), so two processes can't both create the same trial. |
| **Organisation** | A sponsor (or, later, any other organisation) stored once in core.organisation however many trials it sponsors. Found by an identifier a source gives (core.organisation_identifier; CTIS gives EMA's organisation id, scheme ema-org, ORG-…), else, for a name typed by hand, by the same name (case and spacing ignored). |
| **Trial sponsor** | The link between a trial and one of its sponsors (core.trial_sponsor): one primary sponsor and any number of co-sponsors per trial, any number of trials per organisation. Each transfer makes a source's links match what its record lists now; a sponsor it stops listing is kept as removed, and one a person removed stays removed. Shown on the trial page and at /sponsors. |
| **Trial record** | What one source says about a trial (core.trial_record): the payload as received plus `registered`, the adapter's normalised reading (endpoints, dates, status). A new row only when the content differs from the latest. |
| **Possible duplicate** | Two trials whose identifiers turned up together in one record (core.possible_duplicate). Never merged automatically: a person decides merged or distinct; a merge sets trial.merged_into and keeps both. |
| **File, document, version** | Three layers (ADR-003): core.file holds the bytes once by sha256 (with a text fingerprint to spot the same text in other bytes, e.g. one lay summary filed for two countries; none for a scanned file without text); core.document is the logical document as its source identifies it; core.document_version says which file it had from when, keeping replaced ones. core.trial_document links documents to trials with basis and confidence; a document the source stops listing is unlinked but kept (removal source), and one a person unlinks stays unlinked (removal person). |
| **Gold dataset** | A named, versioned set of hand-checked answers per trial, kept as files in `data/gold/<name>/<version>/` (ADR-003), never in the database: dataset.json says what it is, which requirement set its labels answer and which documents its reviewers read (the evidence scope, with the day they read them); items.jsonl holds one gold item per line. Built by a converter per source (`trialwatch gold build bruckner`, src/trialwatch/gold/bruckner.py; run it on the host, as the app container sees data/ read-only); read and searched by src/trialwatch/gold/datasets.py and the web interface's Gold page (the container reads them from TRIALWATCH_GOLD_DIR); a check run is scored against one by `trialwatch gold compare` (src/trialwatch/gold/compare.py). Bruckner et al. is the first. |
| **Gold item** | One trial in a gold dataset: its identifiers (any scheme), the expected parts and label, each reviewer's answers and comments, and the source's own details. An item without a label (e.g. no document to read) stays searchable but is not compared. |
| **Version lock** | data/gold/versions.lock: the sha256 of both files of every released gold dataset version. A released version never changes (`trialwatch gold build` refuses, `trialwatch gold datasets` and a test report any drift); a corrected or larger dataset is a new version. The one exception to "no versioning before production". |
| **JSONB** | Postgres column type for storing and querying JSON documents. |
| **Search export** | The CSV the CTIS public search lets you download: one row per trial, human-readable labels. Bruckner et al.'s exports of 2025-11-10 are in data/bruckner/; the due-date test reads them there. |
| **Result document** | A document of a kind that reports results (core.document_kind has_results): a summary of results, a lay summary, a study report, a publication. From CTIS: the summary of results (type 103), the lay summary (type 46) and the clinical study report (type 45), usually PDFs; types 104 and 7 are the protocol, and any other type is "other". An evidence transfer (`trialwatch source transfer ctis`) downloads the kinds the source's config names into core.document / core.document_version / core.file (the bytes, keyed by sha256); `--files-dir` also writes each one to disk (never overwritten or removed). |
| **Document version** | CTIS gives each document a uuid that stays the same across versions and a systemVersion that goes up when the sponsor replaces the file. CTIS serves only the current version, so every version we download is kept: a version published and replaced between two of our transfers is gone. Each transfer downloads the current file of every listed document again and compares its bytes with the version we hold, so a file replaced without a new systemVersion is a new version too (the label then repeats; the file's sha256 tells them apart). Its published_at is CTIS's submission date of the results it belongs to. |
| **Presigned link** | A temporary download URL with its permission built in. CTIS answers a document download with one pointing at its file storage (Amazon S3), valid for about 5 minutes, so the file is fetched straight away. |
| **Setup** | A named way of running the evidence check: a file in src/trialwatch/checks/configs/ (`default`, `whole-files`) holding the model and its effort, the system prompt, the requirement set, the check method and the retrieval settings (pages per question, the size up to which documents are sent whole). The CLI (`check evidence --config`) and the web forms choose a setup by name and nothing else; `check configs` lists them. One current text per file, like prompts and requirement sets (src/trialwatch/checks/settings.py reads and checks it); each run records the setup's name and every value it used in checks.run, so editing a file never changes what an earlier run says. |
| **Check method** | How the evidence check gives a trial's documents to the model (a setup's `method`): `text`, their extracted page text, searched per question and asked again with every page when needed; or `files`, each document's file sent whole (PDF), so the model also reads scanned pages and charts. Both use the same requirement set, prompt, answer format, quote verification (against the page text), rules, tables and trace. (The `files` method replaces the separate whole-document check.) |
| **System prompt** | The fixed instructions sent with every call of the check: src/trialwatch/checks/prompts/evidence.md (src/trialwatch/checks/model.py loads it). Not versioned before production (ADR-004): there is one current text, and every run stores the exact text it sent with its sha256, so an earlier run always shows its own. It names no registry, document type or regulation; the trial, its documents and the questions are given per call from data, each fenced in tags (see Fenced page text). |
| **Fenced page text** | In a model call, each document's pages sit inside `<document>` and `<page>` tags (src/trialwatch/checks/evidence.py). Text in a PDF or a title that looks like one of those tags (`<page`, `</document`) has its `<` replaced, so a document can't end its page early and speak to the model as if it were our instructions. |
| **Requirement set** | What a trial must report and how "enough" is decided, as data (ADR-004): a JSON file in src/trialwatch/checks/requirement_sets/ (first: eu-ctr-results.json). It names the document kinds to read, the requirements (a question, or one per item of the registered information such as each primary endpoint, with search hints and a rule type: count_stated, statement_present or numeric_result), the parts they count towards and how the parts make a label. src/trialwatch/checks/requirements.py reads it; the web interface's Requirements page shows every set. Not versioned before production; each run stores the set it read with its sha256. |
| **Effort** | How hard the model thinks before answering (low, medium, high, xhigh, max): more effort costs more tokens. Set in a setup and stored with each run. |
| **Refusal fallback** | If the model's safety checks decline a request, the API can re-run it on another model Anthropic recommends (`fallbacks: "default"`); the answer stores which model actually served it. |
| **Don't know** | An answer the model may give to any question of a check (`found`: `dont_know`, stored as checks.evidence.found NULL) when it can't tell: an ambiguous passage, documents that contradict each other, unreadable text. Asked again with every page if it came from the retrieved pages only; after that it is undecided, for a person. Unlike "not found", it never counts as "not reported". |
| **False accusation** | Our check says a trial's results are not fully reported where the reviewers found them full. The costliest error (ADR-001), counted separately by `trialwatch gold compare`. |
| **Set aside** | A gold item that `trialwatch gold compare` leaves out of the score because our check read a document version posted on or after the day the dataset's reviewers read theirs (`evidence.as_of` in dataset.json): ours and theirs may be different documents, so a disagreement would say nothing about the check. |
| **Evidence-based judgement** | src/trialwatch/checks/evidence.py, run by `trialwatch check evidence CT…` (or `--all`) or from the web interface, on trials whose evidence is ready (see Evidence state): for a trial of the central model, the questions come from a requirement set and the trial's registered information (e.g. one per primary endpoint), the pages from every current document of the kinds the set reads linked to it, the model only finds and quotes evidence, code checks every quote against the page text, and the set's rules decide. Each judgement records its inputs at that moment (the trial record and document versions) and its parts by name. Anything the rules can't settle is "undecided" and goes to a person, never a guess: "not reported" needs the whole document read, so a page that is not readable (a scanned PDF), a linked document not read yet, or a negative seen only in retrieved pages leaves a question undecided. |
| **Page text** | The text of each page of a stored file (core.page), extracted once per file with pypdf (src/trialwatch/sources/pages.py) by the transfer or upload that brings the file in. Only PDFs are read. What retrieval searches and what quotes are checked against. |
| **Readable page** | A page counts as read only when its text layer holds at least 200 letters (src/trialwatch/checks/verify.py). A scanned page whose only text is a header or "Page 4 of 10" holds content the model never saw, so "not found" can't mean "not reported" there: the question stays undecided. |
| **Retrieval** | Picking the pages a question needs before the model reads them (src/trialwatch/checks/retrieval.py): Postgres full-text search over the page text, per question, with the words of the requirement's search hints and of the registered item it is about (e.g. the endpoint). Small documents (up to 15 pages) are sent whole; a question not answered from retrieved pages, or an endpoint found there only as not reported, is asked again with every page ("widening"), so a retrieval miss never becomes "not reported". |
| **Verified quote** | Evidence whose quote really occurs on the page it cites (src/trialwatch/checks/verify.py): at least 10 characters long and found on that page as whole words, ignoring case, spacing, dash and quote-mark variants, and hyphens that break a word at the end of a line. So a short token such as "NA" or "0" can't verify by turning up inside another word or number. Only verified quotes count for a judgement. Also called grounding: every claim tied to its source passage. |
| **Judgement run** | One run of the evidence check (checks.run): who ran it, model, effort, the prompt and requirement set it used (their exact texts and sha256), retrieval settings, and how it ended (succeeded, failed, or stopped early, e.g. on bad credentials). Its questions, evidence, judgements and trace are new rows, never updates, so each run is a version; checks.judgement_current shows the newest judgement per trial and requirement set from a run that has ended, and whether it is outdated. |
| **Outdated judgement** | A current judgement (checks.judgement_current) whose inputs changed after it was made: a document version it read was replaced or unlinked, a document of the kinds its requirement set reads was linked since, or another record of the trial came in after its run started. It still shows, marked outdated; check the trial again for a judgement of the evidence as it is now. |
| **Advisory lock** | A lock Postgres keeps for the session that takes it, until it lets go or the session ends (src/trialwatch/db/core.py). TrialWatch uses them so that one process at a time reads a source and one decides trial identities, and every ingest run and check run holds one while it is alive. |
| **Abandoned run** | An ingest run or check run still marked running whose process died, e.g. when the worker was stopped mid-job (the job itself is queued again once its heartbeat stops). Its advisory lock ended with its session, so the next run of its kind (an ingest run, or a check run) sees that and closes it as failed; what it did so far is kept. |
| **Trace** | Every step of judging one trial in one run (checks.step), in order: the questions, the documents and pages read, retrieval (search terms, and per page found its rank, the terms it matched and a snippet), each model call (the questions, the request exactly as sent: the message with the pages and questions, the required answer schema and the settings; the model that answered, tokens, time and its raw answer), quote verification and the rules. The system prompt is stored once per run. Shown step by step on the trial page of the web interface; the Prompts page lists every prompt file and every prompt text runs used. |
| **Web interface** | `trialwatch web` (src/trialwatch/web/app.py): a local site on port 8010. It queues the workflow steps as jobs that the worker runs, and shows their live progress (web/jobs.py): sync a source's list, transfer evidence (records, documents, page text), run the evidence check; changes are recorded as the person who started them. It shows every trial with its identifiers, sources, documents and history (who added what, when), each run's judgement with its trace and quotes marked on the page, "check this trial again" with other settings, and every run with its prompt and settings. Its queries are in src/trialwatch/db/overview.py. Anyone not signed in sees only its home page; every other page and every action needs a signed-in admin or moderator (ADR-006, Role). It answers only requests addressed to this machine (127.0.0.1, localhost, ::1, plus the names in TRIALWATCH_HOSTS) and takes form posts only from its own pages. A stored PDF opens in the browser's viewer, sandboxed; any other file is downloaded. Uploads must be PDFs. |
| **ADR** | Architecture Decision Record: one page per decision (question, options, trade-offs, choice, when to revisit, lab). Ours are Excalidraw pages in docs/adr/, kept in the repo only (not on the docs site). |
| **Verdict confidence** | Not the model's own certainty: see Confidence (of a judgement). ADR-001 first set a line of 99%; ADR-007 replaced it with publishing at 90% measured on gold items, over at least 20 (src/trialwatch/checks/publishing.py). |
| **Manual approval** | A moderator or admin publishes a judgement from its trial page. Needed when its confidence is below 90%, not measured (fewer than 20 gold items) or the label is undecided; /review lists what waits. |
| **Docs check** | tests/test_docs.py: fails when the docs name a file, table or command that doesn't exist, or leave one out, when a diagram's image in docs/img/ is stale, when a diagram has no entry in the site nav, or when a link between diagrams doesn't resolve. Runs with pytest and as a Stop hook. |
| **Pre-commit hook** | A check git runs before each commit, managed by the pre-commit tool (.pre-commit-config.yaml). Ours re-renders the diagram images (tools/diagrams/render.mjs) and stops the commit if one changed. |
| **Docs site** | The website built from README.md, GLOSSARY.md and the architecture diagrams by MkDocs Material (the decisions stay in the repo only) (mkdocs.yml, pages generated by tools/site_hooks.py). Every push to main publishes it to GitHub Pages: https://shmarlya.github.io/trialwatch/. |
| **Answer key** | Bruckner et al.'s final verdict per trial (on time, late, not fully reported, extended, cancelled) and what its results document contained (full, incomplete, none), in their final dataset file in data/bruckner/, plus each reviewer's answers per part in their merged reviews file. Both are in the gold dataset bruckner/2026-03-16. ADR-001: our checks must reach the same verdicts. |
| **Per-part review** | How Bruckner et al.'s reviewers judged each results document: does it report participant numbers, does it give primary outcome results, was the trial cancelled. Two reviewers per document, a third for 37 of them; each gold item keeps every reviewer's answers and comments, and the document's posting date and final comment. |
| **Reviewer agreement** | The first two reviewers gave the same answers before any discussion: 163 of 206 documents (79%). Disagreements went to a third reviewer or a team discussion. |
| **U+FFFD** | The Unicode replacement character (�). Bruckner et al.'s merged reviews file is not valid UTF-8, so its 749 broken byte sequences are read as U+FFFD; only free text is affected. |
| **Due-date rules** | src/trialwatch/checks/due_date.py: Bruckner et al.'s rules for which trials are due and whether results came on time, in plain code, no LLM. tests/test_due_date.py checks that they reproduce all 277 of their verdicts from their files in data/bruckner/. |
